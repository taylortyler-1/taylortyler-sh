Hello, world. 

I'm Taylor Tyler. Currently, I'm the Head of Product at Steppingblocks and a weekend builder of all sorts of things including my open-source agent runtime Automata. 

I spend a lot of my time figuring out what software should do and a little time actually making it do that.

Turns out there's plenty to write about in between. 

## So, what's this about?

I like product management. I don't like some things that come with it. 

Figuring out what customers actually need, working through difficult tradeoffs, building something useful with a talented team? That's the good stuff.

Writing the same requirements in three different places, keeping a pile of tickets updated, and sitting through meetings that probably should've been a conversation? Not so much.

As I build out a product organization of my own, I've been experimenting with how AI can make that second category a little less painful. 

AI shouldn't be making product decisions for us. But it can write a mean Linear ticket with good ACs. 

There are some pretty amazing tools available right now, and we should be using them to make our jobs easier. 

That doesn't mean twice as many PRDs, features, etc. That means handling the tedious stuff and focusing on making a better product or just getting your afternoon back to do what you enjoy. 

Both outcomes sound pretty good to me. 

## What you'll find here

I'll be writing about product management, AI agents, software engineering, and the stuff I'm tinkering with on the weekend.

Some posts will be practical. I'll share workflows I'm using, tools I've built, and experiments with AI that might be useful to other people doing this work.

Others will be about product decisions, engineering tradeoffs, and the things I've learned from getting stuff wrong.

I also want to do some actual testing. There's no shortage of people telling you what AI can supposedly do. I'm more interested in trying it, seeing what happens, and figuring out whether it's worth using.

I don't have some grand framework for the future of product management. I'll leave that to Marty. I'm just trying to make the parts of the job that suck a little less painful while still building good software.

Some of what I try will work. Some of it definitely won't. I'll write about both.

Anyway, that's the plan. Thanks for reading. 

Welcome to the blog.
